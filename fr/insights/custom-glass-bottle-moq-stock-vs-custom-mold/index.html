Bouteille existante ou moule sur mesure ?

Séparez la quantité minimale du flacon, de la fermeture et de chaque opération de décor. Un modèle existant peut limiter le besoin d’outillage, sans supprimer les minimums de couleur ou d’impression. Confirmez le début du délai et les étapes d’approbation dans l’offre.

There is no single minimum order quantity that applies to every glass bottle project. A stock bottle with a standard closure follows a different production route from a coated bottle, a custom-color run or a new mold. The useful question is not only “What is your MOQ?” but “Which part of this specification sets the MOQ?”

Three sourcing routes to compare

  • Stock bottle and stock closure: usually the fastest route to samples and the lowest-risk way to validate capacity, neck finish, filling and packing. Availability and reorder continuity still need confirmation for the exact model.
  • Stock bottle with custom decoration: keeps the proven bottle structure while adding coating, frosting, screen printing, hot stamping, decals or labels. The decoration process may introduce its own setup quantity and color-approval step.
  • Custom bottle mold: supports a proprietary silhouette, embossing, debossing or project-specific geometry. It requires drawings, technical review, tooling, pilot samples and a production quantity that justifies the mold and line setup.

What can move the MOQ

Glass color, bottle weight, neck finish, closure, decoration coverage, artwork colors, carton configuration and destination can all change the feasible starting quantity. A bottle may be available, for example, while its requested cap or decoration needs a different production minimum. Treat the bottle, closure and finish as one quoted system.

Build a quote that can be compared

Ask suppliers to separate the bottle, closure, decoration, mold or tooling, sample and packing assumptions. Also identify whether the quote uses current stock, a future production run or a project-specific mold. This makes alternatives easier to compare and reduces the risk of approving a price for a configuration that was never technically matched.

Selection considerations

Use stock bottles to validate the pack architecture before committing to custom tooling when the shape is still flexible. If a proprietary form is essential, define capacity, target weight, neck finish, decoration area, filling constraints and case packing before the mold brief is frozen.

Questions to resolve before sampling

  • Is a stock bottle acceptable, or does the brand require a proprietary shape or embossing?
  • Which bottle, closure, decoration or packing step is likely to set the minimum quantity?
  • Should the quote separate stock availability, tooling, decorated samples and bulk production assumptions?
Procurement note: MOQ is configuration-specific. Confirm the exact bottle model, glass color, closure, decoration, quantity and destination before treating any starting quantity as applicable to the project.

What to send with an inquiry

Share the application or formula, capacity, material preference, closure or dispensing component, decoration, estimated quantity, destination country, target timing and any reference drawings or photos. Final specifications, MOQ, availability and testing requirements are confirmed for the selected configuration.

Primary references and scope

  • ASTM glass-container standards index — Lists active methods covering glass-container sampling, internal pressure, thermal shock and polariscopic examination.
  • ISO 2859-1:2026 acceptance-sampling overview — Defines AQL-indexed sampling schemes for lot-by-lot inspection by attributes.
  • ASTM D4169 distribution testing overview — Provides a framework for evaluating shipping units against distribution hazards.

Technical standards can inform sampling and performance planning, but they do not set a supplier MOQ, mold charge or lead time. Those commercial inputs must be confirmed for the selected bottle, decoration, quantity and production route.
